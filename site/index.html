A review, start to finish

From diff to published review.

  1. Open a branch or pull request

    The diff comes from your own Git, compared from the merge base.

  2. Comment on any line

    Comments save as you type and stay on your computer.

  3. Add an AI reviewer, or don't

    Your own model reads the change and raises findings, and can double-check them before you see them.

  4. Publish when you're ready

    Comments go to your pending review on GitHub. Nothing is submitted until you say so.

Everything a review needs. Nothing it doesn't.

Comment on any line

Click a line number, write, done. Comments save as you type and stay on your computer.

Bring your own model

OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, OpenRouter, or a local model in Ollama or LM Studio. Reviewers can pull extra context from your MCP servers. Or skip AI entirely.

Press ⌘P, go anywhere

Pull requests waiting on you, recent reviews and branches, all a few keystrokes away.

Pinned to exact commits

Reviews compare from the merge base, so the diff doesn't move when someone pushes.

See what's left

Narrow comments to yours, the AI's, not posted yet or replied to on GitHub, and threads to open or resolved. Your filters stay put next time.

⌘/Ctrl-click to the definition

Jump from any name in the diff to where it's defined, or Shift-click to list its uses. Files outside the change open at the reviewed commit.

Nothing posts by itself

New comments wait in your pending review on GitHub until you publish, with progress as they go. You choose when to submit.

Answer threads in place

Reply to a GitHub thread or resolve it right from the diff. Replies go out when you send them, like on GitHub.

Reviews at full speed.

Diffs come straight from your local Git, so switching files and commits never waits on a page to load.

  • macOS Apple silicon · .dmg
  • macOS Intel · .dmg
  • Windows 10 and 11 · .exe
  • Linux x64 · .AppImage

On macOS, Homebrew works too.

brew install hamedghaderi/tap/review
Your system warns on first open. Here's why.

The installers aren't signed with an Apple or Microsoft certificate yet, so your system asks once:

  • macOS: open Review, then go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.
  • Windows: on the SmartScreen warning, click More info, then Run anyway.
  • Linux: run chmod +x Review-*.AppImage, then open the file.
